# The Wave Praxis

### An open working specification for Waves as adaptive building blocks

**Version:** 0.3 - Guide service and Wave One edition  
**Status:** A proposed direction for Waves.Fund, not a description of features or partnerships already launched  
**Core practice:** Trust People. And They Become Trustworthy.

> A Wave begins with a person and a purpose. A Wave Guide helps give it form. Other people can carry it, challenge it, improve it, and help move it forward. The Wave keeps its home and its history as it grows.

## A Wave in one minute

**A Wave is a custom home for something you are building, shaped with a real Guide, that people can help and pass along.**

For the person receiving it, three things should be immediately clear:

- **The story:** What are these people building, and why does it matter?
- **The next move:** What is needed now, and what can I do?
- **The outcome:** What happened because people helped?

You share your vision. A Guide helps build your Wave. People contribute, offer ideas, or carry it into their communities. The team acknowledges the help, completes a next move, and shows what changed. The Wave adapts to what the mission needs next.

Someone can understand and act on a public Wave without learning the protocol or creating an account first, wherever the action permits. The detailed tools, records, and integrations support that simple experience.

## The proposition

Waves.Fund can offer a new way to raise money and a new way to build together. A builder arrives with a campaign, mission, goal, vision, story, or question. A human Wave Guide listens, learns what is needed, and designs a **custom Wave** with that builder: a useful building block that can tell the story, coordinate people, invite action, and support an appropriate funding path. The Wave belongs to its builder or team. It is made to change as the work changes.

The long-term possibility is larger than a fundraising page. A Wave could become a portable unit of communication and coordination: one evolving home for an idea, with expressions that travel through websites, creators, communities, partner platforms, and screens. Its journey can show how people contributed, where the work went, and what actually happened. Open standards can make Waves interoperable without making them identical.

This is a design thesis, not a claim that every platform will accept a live Wave or that every Wave will raise money. The first test is whether one real Wave helps a real team make its next right moves.

## First principles

1. **The builder owns the direction.** The initiating person or team controls the mission, public story, permissions, and decisions. A Guide serves that direction; a carrier does not acquire it by sharing the Wave.
2. **Human first, adaptive by design.** The Guide discovers the actual need before selecting tools or shaping the experience. A Wave has a small common foundation and no mandatory visual template.
3. **One home, many paths.** Every Wave has a lasting home and identity. Shares, embeds, features, pitches, and media point back to it, even as many paths branch outward at once.
4. **Movement is visible when people consent.** A Wave's Journey records meaningful, attributable actions. Public history shows approved facts; private work and personal information stay private.
5. **Credit follows evidence.** A feature is not an endorsement. A visit is not a contribution. A pledge is not money received. Recognition describes the actual role and evidence for each outcome.
6. **Support does not buy control.** Paid reach, partner funding, community feedback, and formal allocation decisions remain distinguishable. Sponsorship is disclosed; a purchase cannot silently determine a vote or funding decision.
7. **Portable means the builder can leave.** The team can export its content, history, and permitted data. Open standards should reduce dependence on any one host, agency, or channel.
8. **Trust is practiced, not claimed.** Changes can be reviewed, permissions can be withdrawn, errors can be corrected with a visible record, and claims of impact can be checked.

## What a Wave is

A Wave combines a public story, useful ways to participate, and a workspace the team can adapt. It has one canonical home—the authoritative place for its current state—and can appear differently in other places while preserving its identity. Private work stays in the team workspace; visitors see a clear story and an appropriate next move.

The shared structure is deliberately small. It answers: **Who is building? Why does this matter? What is happening? What is needed now? How can someone help? What can be verified?** The Guide and builder decide the rest.

| Layer | Minimum shared structure | Room to adapt |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Identity | Stable Wave ID and canonical address; owner/steward; creation and update dates | Name, visual language, voices, languages, accessible formats |
| Story | Purpose, current state, intended change, people affected | Film, audio, writing, diagrams, research, lived experience |
| Action | One primary next move, its owner, needed contribution, and completion condition | Join a team, mentor, donate, apply, purchase, share, propose an idea |
| Evidence | Source, date, and status for public claims and milestones | Field reports, links, receipts, partner attestations, media |
| Participation | Roles, permissions, consent, contribution and review rules | Community discussion, creative collaboration, advisory signals |
| Journey | Dated events with an actor, event type, visibility, and evidence level | Features, introductions, handoffs, updates, funding, delivery |
| Portability | Exportable core data and media references; versioned format | Website embed, social rendition, video, screen, partner adapter |

**Optional modules** might include a grant workspace, fundraising campaign, team board, volunteer intake, public roadmap, research notebook, sales inquiry, event, learning path, or Wave Pool. Modules are added because the builder needs them, not to make every Wave look complete.

### What makes the Wave adaptive

The most useful change is often a change in **what the Wave asks for next**. A team may first need a designer, then equipment, then funds for a test, then someone to review the result. Each stage can keep the same home and history while presenting a different next move.

Every primary next move should name an accountable person, the help needed, an expected response time, and what would count as completion. A deadline is included when it is real. Other ways to help remain available without competing for equal attention.

The builder or authorized Guide acknowledges an offer, confirms whether it fits, and records the agreed action. When evidence shows the need has been met, the team closes it and selects the next move. Offers and commitments do not count as completed work or funds received. If a request changes or cannot be completed, the Wave explains that change and addresses outstanding commitments.

**The return path matters as much as the outward share.** With permission, someone who helped can receive an update showing the contribution acknowledged, the outcome reported, and what is needed next. They choose whether to keep following. The system should carry the context forward so builders and supporters do not have to begin the same conversation again.

### Core invariants for an open protocol

- Every Wave **must** identify its steward and canonical home.
- Every public claim **must** distinguish assertion, independently confirmed evidence, and estimate.
- Every public action **must** identify who may take it and what happens next.
- A person **must** consent before their private contribution or identity is made public.
- An accepted holding role **must** identify the recipient, permitted use, duration, and whether paid placement is involved. Ordinary sharing of a public link does not create that role.
- A correction **must** preserve the fact that an earlier public version existed without perpetuating harmful private data.
- A platform adapter **must** state what it can publish or read; it must not imply a connection it does not have.
- A Wave **should** remain useful without any single social network, payment provider, or blockchain.

## The Wave Guide: the human in the loop

The Guide is the person who helps the builder move from an intention to a working Wave. The relationship is collaborative, not a transfer of agency.

One Guide should remain accountable for the relationship even when designers, developers, funding specialists, or other Guides contribute. The builder should know who will respond and when. The Guide need not personally hold every specialty.

1. **Listen and map.** Learn the mission, people, stage, constraints, existing work, funding needs, and the next meaningful action. Ask what the builder does *not* want made public.
2. **Shape the Wave.** Write a brief with the builder: story, audience, action, evidence, design, modules, accessibility, consent, and success measures. Confirm scope and ownership before building.
3. **Build and connect.** Create the mobile experience and useful tools; connect approved services and platforms only with the builder's authorization. Keep a usable export and a record of decisions.
4. **Coordinate the journey.** Invite collaborators, review proposed changes, prepare channel-specific versions, and help people pass the Wave responsibly.
5. **Guide the funding route.** Distinguish grants, donations, sponsorships, sales, debt, and investment. Help with research, readiness, applications, and introductions within the Guide's actual qualifications. Bring in appropriately authorized specialists where required.
6. **Evolve and hand over.** Review outcomes with the builder, improve the Wave, document what changed, and make sure the team or a successor Guide can operate it. The handover includes decisions, open commitments, permissions, source ownership, exports, and the next move.

A future Guide profile could show areas of practice, verified projects served, builder feedback, and outcomes. If it reports money, it should distinguish **committed from received**, **led from assisted**, and **attributed through a path from independently verified**. A large number alone is not a measure of care or quality. Guides should disclose fees, referral relationships, and conflicts.

### A service people can understand and afford

A builder hires a human Wave Guide to design and build their own Wave. They may agree on a **defined build**, a **weekly engagement**, or **monthly support**. Scope and price are agreed directly before work begins; this specification sets no rates and offers no automatic checkout or funding guarantee.

The first engagement should promise a defined deliverable: a working Wave that supports one agreed next move, with a clear scope, price or subsidy, delivery window, revision allowance, and handover. A recurring engagement also names the included work, response times, review rhythm, renewal and cancellation terms, and treatment of unfinished work. Hosting, third-party charges, continuing Guide support, and paid distribution are visible, separate choices. This gives builders a way to start without committing to unlimited custom development.

Reuse the foundations that work; spend human time on understanding, judgment, creative choices, and relationships. The pilot should record Guide hours, delivery cost, builder time saved, and maintenance burden. Partner-sponsored builds could extend access to lifelong learners with limited budgets; they would require actual funding and transparent eligibility. An open standard makes it possible for other Guides and hosts to serve a Wave as well.

## A team around the current move

A Wave brings people together around a **shared current focus**: what the team is trying to accomplish now, who is accountable, what help fits, and how completion will be recognized. The wider mission provides continuity; the current focus makes joining useful and manageable.

The builder or steward sets direction. The accountable Guide helps turn it into a working experience. A move owner coordinates a particular action. Contributors accept specific tasks or offers, while carriers help the Wave reach people. A funder or supporter does not automatically receive editorial control or private team access. One person can hold several roles when those responsibilities are explicit.

Joining should feel simple: understand the story, choose an open role or offer help, agree on the next action, and receive only the access that action needs. The team acknowledges the offer and records its owner, expected effort, dependencies, and response time. People can follow, leave, or hand over a role without losing credit for completed work. Public recognition and future updates remain choices.

A short shared record connects decisions, active commitments, and results. It helps a new teammate understand what matters without repeating every conversation. The Guide reviews this record with the team as the focus changes, closes fulfilled requests, and makes the next invitation clear.

## Pass the Wave

Anyone can share a public Wave link. Accepting an invitation to **hold a Wave** adds an agreed responsibility: perhaps a feature, introduction, event, or period of active support. The builder keeps the Wave's home. A recipient may add a perspective, propose an edit, publish a feature, introduce a partner, or invite support. The builder decides which changes become part of the canonical Wave.

The talking-stick image offers a memorable social ritual: *I have listened; I will carry this forward; I will show what I did.* The digital system can support multiple branches at once. It may name a **current featured holder** for a campaign or event, while preserving the broader network of active carriers. Holding a Wave is a temporary role, not ownership, exclusivity, authority to make financial promises, or access to private data.

A pass moves through **invited → accepted → active → completed, declined, expired, or revoked**. Its record includes the parties, scope, channel, period, disclosure requirements, and any agreed review of the message. A recipient can publish their own commentary without silently rewriting the builder's words. Proposed changes enter a review queue; acceptance creates a new version with credit.

The recipient receives a short, approved handoff: the story, the current next move, the claims they can use, the agreed role, and the return link. This can adapt to their audience while preserving the builder's meaning. A pass can be declined without a penalty; the interface makes the commitment clear before acceptance.

Each feature links to the version it presented and to the Wave's current state. Later edits must not silently rewrite an earlier funding promise or pitch. Revoking a role stops future authorized actions and access; it cannot guarantee removal of copies already published elsewhere.

### The Journey

The Journey is a readable timeline and branching map of the Wave in motion. Events can include a Guide match, published update, creator feature, community discussion, introduction, application, funding commitment, confirmed receipt, project milestone, and correction. Each event carries a timestamp, source, visibility level, and evidence state: **self-reported, linked, partner-confirmed, or transaction-verified**.

A creator's feature might show its published link and date, its tracked visits to the Wave, and funds received through a distinct route. That is **path attribution**, not proof that the creator alone caused every gift. On-platform impressions need authorized platform data; private donor identities do not become public Journey entries. The public view may celebrate a carrier's contribution while private analytics preserve more detail for the builder.

**Example:** A student team builds a Wave with a Guide. A YouTuber accepts a feature pass and publishes a video. Viewers follow the video's Wave link; some mentor, some suggest a design change, and some give through the approved funding route. The Journey shows the confirmed feature, the proposed change and the team's decision, and the verified funds received through that route. The team then publishes what it built. The Wave has become a record of collective work, not merely a counter of views.

### The experience should make care visible

On a phone, the opening view should make the story, current need, and primary action easy to understand. The fuller Journey is one deliberate step away. People should see plain language such as “Offer help,” “Pass the Wave,” or “See what changed”; they do not need to understand a manifest, adapter, or verification scheme.

Motion should communicate real changes: a person accepts an invitation, the team acknowledges help, a need is met, or a result returns to its contributors. Use the supplied Waves identity, flowing cyan and electric-blue media, readable white text, feathered surfaces, and restrained transitions. Honor reduced-motion preferences and keep every action available through a clear button. The experience should still make sense with animation paused.

The feeling to design for is personal and specific: *They understood my idea. I know how to help. They remembered my contribution. I can see what happened.*

## Funding and Wave Pools

Funding is one possible action within a Wave, not its sole purpose. A Wave may need people, knowledge, a customer, an introduction, equipment, or time more than money. Its funding path should state the recipient, purpose, mechanism, fees, current status, and who is accountable for reporting back.

| Route | What the participant is doing | What the Wave must make clear |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Donation | Giving to an identified recipient | Purpose, provider, fees, receipt issuer, and any documented tax treatment; no implied ownership or return |
| Grant | Applying for or awarding support under stated terms | Eligibility, decision maker, restrictions, milestones, reporting, and award status |
| Sponsorship | Paying for an agreed association or deliverable | Deliverables, disclosures, term, approvals, and how completion is confirmed |
| Sale or service | Buying a product or commissioned work | Seller, price, delivery, cancellation or refund terms, and what is included |
| Debt or investment | Entering a financing arrangement | Actual legal instrument, rights and obligations, risks, eligibility, and appropriately qualified counterparties |

These routes have different promises and records. A Guide fee pays for agreed work; a donation supports its named recipient; an investment follows its own legal terms. The interface identifies which action a person is taking before they proceed. Money passes through the designated provider or recipient, and public totals distinguish pledged, received, refunded, and disbursed amounts. Unconnected providers cannot supply automatic totals. The first pilot uses confirmed records and labels any manual reconciliation.

A **Wave Pool** is a proposed optional module for people or organizations supporting a shared goal. The first, simplest pool may coordinate **nonfinancial resources**: mentor hours, space, equipment, or expertise. Several Waves might need the same resource; a shared offer could help them together and reduce what each needs to raise. Each team must accept its allocation, and finite resources must not be promised twice. A donation or grant pool would need an identified legal recipient, eligibility rules, allocation process, records, and an approved payment/disbursement provider. A pooled investment structure would be a separate, later product with qualified legal and regulated partners. The name “pool” must never imply that Waves.Fund already holds, invests, or allocates money.

Every financial pool proposal should define: who can contribute; who controls the funds; who decides allocation; eligibility and conflicts; how funds are received and released; fees; reporting and appeals; what happens if the goal fails; and what evidence is public. A student support signal may help inform a decision, but it is not automatically a verified ballot or legal authorization to disburse funds.

The future investment layer can explore opportunities, research, introductions, and collective diligence, but it must be designed separately from charitable giving. Securities solicitation, transaction-based compensation, investment advice, and custody can require registration and specialized controls. Waves.Fund should work with qualified partners before offering those functions. No Wave promises funding or returns.

## The Wave as communication, coordination, and growth

A Wave can carry the story *and* the work. It gives someone a coherent way to understand a mission, see its progress, choose a useful action, and follow what happens next. This can serve a community campaign, nonprofit, venture, artist, research project, product launch, or local initiative.

For growth and marketing, the Wave is a source of truth from which approved channel versions are made: a website experience, a link preview, an embed, a social post, a creator brief, a short film, or a display for a screen. These are **expressions of one Wave**, not promises that arbitrary interactive code can run inside every platform. Paid placement is labeled clearly and remains separate from earned community support or review decisions.

For sales, a Wave might invite a conversation, demonstrate work, gather qualified inquiries, and show delivery over time. For capital raising, it might organize a credible story, evidence, team, funding need, and permitted route to a qualified partner. For coordination, it might hold the next actions, owners, decisions, and updates. The same Wave can change emphasis as its mission advances.

## Interoperability: an open foundation, many adapters

The proposed **Wave format** should be published as an open, versioned specification after real builds show what is truly common. Its minimum components are a Wave manifest, portable content, module declarations, permissions, Journey event vocabulary, and export rules. A reference implementation should demonstrate how to create, render, pass, and export a Wave. Extensions should be namespaced and optional so a custom Wave does not have to fit a universal page layout.

Integration proceeds in levels:

| Level | Capability | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Share | Canonical link and accurate preview | A social post points to the Wave |
| 2. Render | Permissioned embed or channel-specific media | A partner site displays an approved Wave view |
| 3. Publish | Authorized account connection | The builder approves a platform post |
| 4. Exchange | Approved two-way data flow | Confirmed campaign data returns to the Journey |
| 5. Transact | Qualified provider completes a financial action | A receipt confirms funds received |

Adapters for X, LinkedIn, Facebook, YouTube, fundraising platforms, or a DAO should be built only against their available permissions and policies. Some destinations allow a link, some a post or embed, and some may offer an approved API. The protocol can remain universal in **meaning and ownership** without assuming universal access to every platform. Open web conventions such as [Open Graph](https://ogp.me/) and [ActivityPub](https://www.w3.org/TR/activitypub/) can inform particular routes; they do not by themselves provide every desired integration.

## Ownership, safety, and verification

The builder owns its authored content and should receive exportable data and source for the custom work as agreed in the build contract. Publishing a common protocol or reference implementation as open source does **not** automatically publish a team's private content or custom code. Licensing choices belong in plain-language agreements before work begins.

The Wave needs clear permissions for team members, Guides, carriers, and platform connections; accessible rendering; backup and recovery; data minimization; removal requests; and a way to correct false claims. A Journey record can be signed or made tamper-evident. A blockchain might later anchor proofs between independent parties, but an immutable record cannot establish that its underlying claim is true and is a poor home for private identities or donor data. Verification begins with accountable people, source evidence, and honest labels.

Creator sponsorships and paid placements require clear disclosure. Funding receipts come from the actual payment or funding provider. Any metrics shared publicly should state their source and limits. No estimated reach, commitment, or support signal should be presented as money raised.

## An honest path from thesis to protocol

### Wave One: Waves.Fund

**Story:** Students Funding The Future. An Impact Fund. Funding For Futures. A place for lifelong learners and leaders to bring a vision, find human guidance, and build together.

**Founder and first Wave Guide:** August James Domanchuk, founder of Outlier.Systems and Semble.CC; an Emergent Strategist, a life long learner and teacher, musician, and regenerative systems researcher. His core mantra is **Move The Mindset**. The Waves.Fund core practice is **Trust People. And They Become Trustworthy.**

**Home:** [Waves.Fund](https://waves.fund/). Its existing application, team review, consented public project feed, advisory learner support, and external giving route provide the starting point. This example presents the platform itself as the first Wave; it does not imply a newly funded project or an executed Guide agreement.

**Proposed current focus:** Build and test the first complete Guide-led Wave pilot. August would help a builder shape one useful next move, agree the engagement, create the custom experience, and invite one consenting carrier to help it travel.

**How people can help now:** Share a project through the existing application, express interest in becoming a Guide, or contact August about a pilot or partnership. Guide engagements and pilot participation need a direct conversation and agreed terms.

**Completion evidence for the proposed pilot:** A builder-approved working Wave, an agreed action delivered, a consented record of the outcome, and a usable export and handover. Share lessons and reusable structures only with permission. No funding amount, contract, completed handoff, or platform integration is asserted by this example.

**Pilot - complete one useful cycle.** Begin with one builder, one accountable Guide, one clear need, and one invited carrier. Help reaches the team, the team accepts it, a next move is completed, and the result returns to the people who opted to follow it. Use an existing provider for any real payment. Then try two different Wave types to learn which parts remain useful and which need to adapt.

**Extract — publish what repeats.** Identify the smallest shared structure found across those builds. Release a draft Wave format, export, accessibility criteria, and reference implementation. With the team's permission, preserve reusable modules and a short account of the steps, resources, results, and lessons. Include unsuccessful approaches and their context. A later builder could begin with a tested path and adapt it with a Guide; private histories stay private, and previous success is never a guarantee. Invite independent builders and Guides to challenge the format.

**Connect — grow through consent.** Add embeds, media generation, authorized publishing, and selected partner adapters. Give builders control over each connection. Seek deeper platform partnerships after real use demonstrates value.

**Expand — add accountable funding services.** Develop Guide practice standards, verified outcome profiles, and carefully governed Wave Pools. Work with qualified partners for regulated financial paths. Publish actual results and limitations before making large claims.

### How to decide whether the pilot worked

Measure the complete participation cycle: a useful request was understood, an offer was accepted, the promised action was delivered, and an outcome was reported. Record where people stopped and why. Keep fulfilled actions, referrals, funds received, and reported project outcomes distinct.

Ask recipients to explain the mission and next move in their own words before giving them a walkthrough. Check that the builder can change the next request, correct a claim, pause participation, and export the Wave. Have a successor Guide use the handover to understand unfinished work. Record builder time saved, Guide hours, delivery costs, and upkeep so the service can remain viable.

A completed Wave should state what was achieved, what remains unresolved, and how outstanding commitments will be handled. It can be paused or closed without manufacturing continuing activity. Its public record can remain available under the team's publication choices; a new chapter is a deliberate decision.

### Questions the pilot must answer

- What minimum structure helps every builder without flattening their story?
- What does a Guide build once, and what must be shaped with each person?
- Who can carry a Wave, for how long, and how is permission withdrawn?
- Which Journey events can be verified without exposing people or creating surveillance?
- How do we credit collective effort without pretending one link caused an entire outcome?
- Which parts should be open source, which should be owned by the builder, and which are a paid service?
- What evidence would convince another platform to adopt a Wave connection?

### What stays outside the first pilot

The first pilot needs a working Wave, a human Guide, a simple participation path, and evidence of the result. Broad platform partnerships, automated allocation, a public-chain record, and investment pools are later hypotheses. Expand when an observed need and qualified partners justify the work.

**Current product boundary:** The present Waves.Fund application provides private submissions, team review, consented public project publication, advisory learner support, and an external donation route. The adaptive Wave workspace, holding roles, Journey attribution, pools, and protocol described here are proposed capabilities.

## The statement

**Raise Money In A New Way.**

A Wave is not finished when it is published. It grows when someone listens, acts, carries it onward, and gives the work back to its people with more clarity than they found it. The protocol should make that movement possible; the praxis should make it trustworthy.
