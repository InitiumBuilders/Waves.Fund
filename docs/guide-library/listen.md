# Listen

## Begin With The Person

A Wave begins with a person and a purpose. Your first work as a Guide is to understand that purpose well enough that the builder recognizes their own voice in what you make together.

Start with a conversation. Ask permission before recording it. Listen for what already exists, what has been tried, what the builder can sustain, and what people affected by the work actually need. A beautiful experience is useful when it helps someone take a meaningful next step.

## Work Through The Conversation

1. Ask: **What are you building, and why does it matter to you?** Write down their words before suggesting a description.
2. Ask: **Who is this for?** Name the people affected and who can speak to their needs. Find out who should help shape the Wave before it becomes public.
3. Ask: **What is real today?** Collect existing work, links, collaborators, constraints, and evidence. Label a plan as a plan and a result as a result.
4. Ask: **What needs to happen next?** Identify one obstacle or opportunity small enough to address together.
5. Ask: **What can we share?** Separate the public story from contact details, finances, private conversations, and unfinished work.
6. Read the brief back. Invite corrections. Agree how the builder will approve it and who will respond between meetings.

## An Annotated Example

**Growth Wave — The Community Workshop.** This is an illustrative teaching case.

The initial request is “help us grow.” Discovery reveals that the workshop already has tools and interested learners, but nobody available to host the next session. The useful next move becomes **find two workshop hosts**. The public Wave can explain the role and invite offers. The workspace keeps personal availability and contact details private.

**Why this matters:** the brief now connects a real need to an action someone can take. It also avoids claiming that a larger audience alone will make the workshop happen.

## Make The Brief Useful

Write a short public story, name an accountable builder, describe the current state, and list the people who should be consulted. Record the first next move and the evidence that would show it is complete. Add constraints: time, budget, access, language, devices, maintenance, and existing commitments.

Use the discovery worksheet. A brief is ready when the builder can say, “Yes. That is what I mean, and that is what we need next.” Do not publish notes from the conversation as a substitute for approval.

## Guide Tips

- Ask for examples when someone says “community,” “impact,” or “growth.” Make those words concrete together.
- Keep the builder's language. Edit for clarity without replacing their purpose with yours.
- Ask what would make this engagement unsuccessful. That often reveals an important constraint early.
- Treat uncertainty as useful context. A question can be the beginning of a Wave.

## Your Deliverable

A builder-approved discovery brief with a public story, private context, first next move, and a named decision maker.

## Before You Move On

- [ ] The builder recognizes and approves their own story.
- [ ] Existing work and intended outcomes are clearly distinguished.
- [ ] People affected by the work have a clear place in the conversation.
- [ ] Public and private information are identified.
- [ ] One next move and an accountable person are named.
