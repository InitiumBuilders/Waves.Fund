# Adapt And Hand Over

## Let The Wave Change With The Work

An adaptive Wave carries the work forward without making the team begin again. Review what actually happened, close what is complete, and decide what is needed now. The story, tools, and current move can change while the Wave retains its home and approved history.

A Guide's work includes making it possible for the builder or a successor to continue. Ownership and independence are part of a useful service.

## Review The Whole Journey

1. Compare the result with the move's completion condition. Record what was completed, partially completed, or not completed, with evidence and remaining uncertainty.
2. Ask the builder and contributors what helped, what was difficult, and what should change. Seek permission before publishing anyone's feedback.
3. Close or renegotiate outstanding commitments. A new direction does not erase obligations to people already involved.
4. Choose the next move with the builder. Update the public invitation, responsible person, and completion condition.
5. Review the engagement. Agree new scope, fees, and timing before adding substantial work; confirm whether the relationship continues or hands over.
6. Export permitted records and transfer documented access through appropriate secure channels. Never put passwords or service tokens into a public export or handbook.

## An Annotated Example

**Innovation Wave — Open River Sensor.** This is an illustrative teaching case.

A prototype completes a field test. The test reveals useful readings and a calibration issue. The outcome describes the method, observations, and limits. The next move becomes finding a calibration reviewer; it does not claim the sensor is ready for every environment.

**Why this matters:** an honest result gives the next contributor enough context to help. Learning remains valuable even when the first hypothesis does not hold.

## Prepare A Handover Someone Can Follow

Document the canonical home, current version, source location, permissions, account owners, operating costs, update routine, known issues, and recovery process. Include open commitments and the next review date. Check that the builder can use their export and can identify which code or media they own, license, or may share.

Agree the period of ongoing support and what happens after it ends. Remove access that is no longer needed. A new Guide should be able to understand the current state without relying on the previous Guide's memory.

## Make The Next Wave Easier

With permission, turn useful patterns into reusable structures: an intake flow, a parts list, a research method, or a contribution workflow. Remove private information and confirm ownership before sharing. State the license when releasing code or materials for others to use. Learning can travel while individual teams retain their own direction.

## Guide Tips

- Report an incomplete move honestly and explain the next decision.
- Test the handover by asking the builder to perform a routine update.
- Agree maintenance before a feature becomes essential to operations.
- Reuse a pattern because it fits the next builder's need, not because it already exists.

## Your Deliverable

An approved outcome, a new next move or clear closure, and a usable handover with ownership, access, maintenance, and open commitments accounted for.

## Before You Move On

- [ ] The outcome describes completed work and remaining uncertainty.
- [ ] Existing commitments are completed, changed by agreement, or closed.
- [ ] The builder approved the next move or closure.
- [ ] Export, source ownership, access, and maintenance are documented.
- [ ] The builder or successor can complete a routine task using the handover.
