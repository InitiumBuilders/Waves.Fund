# Carry And Report

## Pass The Wave. Bring The Outcome Back.

A Wave has one home and can travel through many people. A creator might feature it, a community might host an event, or a partner might make an introduction. Make the invitation specific and return the result to the people who helped.

Ordinary sharing does not make someone an official carrier. A carrying invitation is an agreed role with a purpose, permitted materials, duration, and clear contact. Carrying the Wave does not transfer ownership or authority to publish changes to its home.

## Prepare A Useful Handoff

1. Share the current story, canonical link, specific next move, and approved materials.
2. Ask the recipient to accept the proposed role. Agree what they intend to do, when, and whether payment or another relationship should be disclosed.
3. Record the invitation status and consent. A declined or expired invitation is not a completed feature.
4. Prepare the version their channel can use: a short description, accessible image, video script, event brief, or ordinary link. Test the actual destination.
5. After the action, collect the permitted evidence: a published link, attendance record, confirmed introduction, or recipient receipt.
6. Review the result with the builder. Publish an approved outcome and provide an update the contributor can access or the team can deliberately share.

## An Annotated Example

**Story Wave — Voices Of The Coast.** This is an illustrative teaching case.

A creator accepts an invitation to feature an approved chapter. Participants agree how their voices and images may be used. The Journey records the published feature, date, and role after review. If a participant withdraws permission, the team reviews what must be removed or changed and coordinates with the places carrying the material.

**Why this matters:** the Wave can travel while the people behind its story retain a voice in how it is told. A feature shows that a story was shared; it does not prove that viewers contributed.

## Keep Credit Honest

Report a share, visit, offer, commitment, completed task, pledge, and money received as separate facts. A tracked link can support a statement about activity through that path; it cannot establish that the creator caused every later contribution.

When reporting funds, name the recipient and reporting period, distinguish pledged from received, and use confirmed records. Show uncertainty where the evidence is incomplete. Keep donor details and private receipts out of public updates unless sharing is appropriate and permitted.

## Guide Tips

- Write a handoff someone can understand without repeating the whole discovery conversation.
- Give carriers a clear way to stop and have their status updated.
- Use dates so old invitations do not appear current.
- Close the circle: **You helped. Here's what changed. Here's what comes next.**

## Your Deliverable

An accepted carrying brief, a documented action, and a builder-approved outcome with appropriate credit and evidence.

## Before You Move On

- [ ] The carrier accepted the purpose, period, and permitted use.
- [ ] Materials and participants' permissions are current.
- [ ] Published facts are supported and distinct from plans or estimates.
- [ ] Contributions and money are attributed only as far as evidence supports.
- [ ] The people who helped have a clear way to see the outcome.
