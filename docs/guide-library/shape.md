# Shape

## Give The Next Move A Clear Form

Your Wave can evolve. Its first version only needs to help the right people understand the story and make the next useful move. Shape the work with the builder before you design the interface or add tools.

Agree on a paid engagement for an external builder: a defined build, weekly engagement, or monthly support. Put the scope, fee, payment schedule, delivery window, review points, ownership, and ongoing care in writing. This library sets no rates. A shared scope record supports the relationship; it does not replace a suitable service agreement.

## Define The Work Together

1. Complete this sentence: **We are building ___ for ___ so they can ___.**
2. Choose one primary next move. Name the person responsible, the help needed, and what completion would look like.
3. List the minimum tools that move needs. A contribution form may be enough; a large dashboard may add work without helping.
4. Specify the first deliverable and what the builder will review. Include an accessible phone experience, content, connections, and handover materials.
5. Agree what is outside the current engagement. Decide how new requests are priced and approved before work begins on them.
6. Agree how to pause or end the engagement, including open work, payments, access, and export.

## An Annotated Example

**Brave Wave: The Community Readiness Brief.** This is an illustrative teaching case.

The first move is **review the access plan before the workshop opens**. The Wave needs the known facts with their sources, the open questions, one named owner for the decision, and the date of the next review. The Guide keeps facts, open questions, and decisions apart, so no one mistakes a guess for a confirmation.

**Why this matters:** a next move with one owner and a visible finish is one people can help with. They can see what is decided, what is still open, and when it will be checked again.

## Choose A Shape, Then Adapt It

Growth, Funding, Innovation, Story, Hype, Maker, Work, and Brave Waves are starting models. The builder may combine aspects or need something different. Use the shared foundation—identity, story, action, evidence, participation, Journey, and export—while customizing the actual experience.

For example, a Maker Wave may begin with a parts request and later need a field test. Keep its home and approved history while changing the current move. Revisit scope if that new move requires substantial new work.

## Guide Tips

- Describe acceptance in observable terms: a person can submit an offer and the team can acknowledge it.
- Record who can approve public changes. A contributor or carrier does not automatically receive publication authority.
- Set a response rhythm the team can maintain. An honest weekly review is better than a promise nobody can meet.
- If funding involves specialized services, bring in a suitably qualified person before committing to that work. Do not promise funding or investment returns.

## Your Deliverable

An agreed engagement and a first-build brief with a clear next move, acceptance conditions, ownership, and review date.

## Before You Move On

- [ ] Builder and Guide have approved the scope and fee arrangement.
- [ ] The primary move has an owner and observable completion condition.
- [ ] The first build includes only the tools needed now.
- [ ] Revision, ownership, maintenance, and handover terms are recorded.
- [ ] Funding needs, Guide fees, and confirmed receipts remain distinguishable.
