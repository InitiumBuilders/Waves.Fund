# Coordinate

## Turn An Offer Into Shared Work

An offer is the beginning of a conversation. A Guide helps the builder respond, agree on a useful contribution, and keep the commitment clear as circumstances change. Someone's time deserves the same care as the public story.

Keep one accountable lead Guide. Supporting Guides may bring design, research, community, funding, or operational experience. Make responsibilities visible so the builder knows who will respond and contributors know whom to contact.

## Carry A Contribution Through

1. Review the offer against the current move. Acknowledge it within the response window the team has agreed.
2. Clarify what the person can offer, what they need, and when they are available. Discuss payment or expenses explicitly where relevant.
3. Agree a specific task, owner, due date if real, and completion evidence. Record whether the contribution may be publicly recognized.
4. Give only the access needed for that contribution. Share private information with permission and a clear reason.
5. Check in at agreed points. If the move changes, tell affected contributors and agree whether to adjust, pause, or close their commitments.
6. Review the delivered work. Record what was accepted, what still needs attention, and how credit will be given.

## An Annotated Example

**Work Wave — The Workshop Opening.** This is an illustrative teaching case.

A contributor offers “help with setup.” The Guide and builder agree on a setup lead, a list of dependent tasks, and a review before the workshop opens. Completion means the assigned setup work has been reviewed and its handoff accepted; remaining work has its own named owner.

**Why this matters:** a clear agreement protects people's time and makes progress observable. The Journey can recognize the completed setup with the contributor's consent without exposing the private discussion.

## Keep The Team On The Same Page

Use a brief recurring review: what changed, what is blocked, whose decision is needed, and what happens next. Record decisions where the team can find them. If two people suggest different directions, bring the choice back to the accountable builder.

A Brave Wave can help a community maintain current context and coordinate care. Keep sensitive reports private, name the responsible reviewer, and publish only the information people need and are permitted to share. A public activity feed is not an appropriate home for an unreviewed allegation or someone's private safety information.

## Guide Tips

- A courteous decline is useful. Explain when an offer does not fit and close it clearly.
- Avoid assigning work to someone who has only expressed interest.
- Make withdrawal and changes of availability straightforward.
- Credit the specific contribution rather than implying ownership of the whole result.

## Your Deliverable

An acknowledged offer, a mutually understood commitment, and a private record of the work, decisions, and consent.

## Before You Move On

- [ ] Every accepted contribution has an owner and agreed deliverable.
- [ ] Payment, expenses, timing, and dependencies are understood.
- [ ] Access matches the person's actual responsibilities.
- [ ] Public recognition has explicit consent.
- [ ] Contributors know how to update or withdraw their commitment.
